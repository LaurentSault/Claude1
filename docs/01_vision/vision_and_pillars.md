## 1. The Game in One Breath

### 1.1 Elevator pitch (30 seconds)

*Final Fantasy VI* in the Paris of Chopin and Delacroix. Kang Min-jun, a 22-year-old Korean composition student, steps through a door beneath his Seoul conservatory on Thu 5 Mar 2026 and wakes in the quarries under Paris on Tue 5 Mar 1833. Playing music nobody has written yet makes him the sensation of the salons and the target of the Anachronists: time-travellers who stole the future to rule the past and will kill him before they let him go home. Befriend geniuses, keep your secret, teach a struggling painter to paint light, and decide with her which century is home.

### 1.2 Logline (canonical, CANON §1, used verbatim)

> In 1833 Paris, a Korean composition student who fell through a door beneath his Seoul conservatory must outplay a cabal of time-travellers who have stolen the future to rule the past and who will kill him before they let him go home, while the painter they sent to keep him there becomes the reason he must choose between two centuries.

**Usage rule.** The canonical logline is for the team, publishers and embargoed press kits: it names Lucile's role as a plant, which the player learns gradually from CH-03 to CH-09 (this doc, §5.5). Consumer-facing copy uses the back-of-box text, which never states that she was sent.

### 1.3 Back-of-box copy

> **PLAY THE FUTURE. KEEP THE SECRET.**
>
> Seoul, 2026. Beneath his conservatory, composition student Kang Min-jun finds a door that should not exist.
> Paris, 1833. He wakes among the bones of the old quarries with a dying phone, a tuning fork and a head full of music no one has written yet.
>
> The city is still mourning the cholera. Its salons are hungry for genius. And somewhere among the patrons and painters, someone already knows exactly who he is, and exactly where he came from.
>
> - **Perform the future.** Debussy, Ravel, Rachmaninoff, Scriabin and Prokofiev, decades before they were written, as three-movement Recitals that turn the tide of battle.
> - **Fight beside legends.** Berlioz summons spectral orchestras, Delacroix paints with fire and wind, Liszt duels with lightning, Chopin bends time itself, Alkan hammers out impossible Études and Louise Farrenc reads every enemy like a score.
> - **Hide in plain sight.** Every dazzling performance feeds the Secrecy Meter. Show off, or stay alive.
> - **Fall in love with a painter of light.** Teach Lucile Aubray to paint what no one in 1833 has seen, and learn what she has been hiding.
> - **Choose a century.** Two complete, playable endings, one in Seoul 2026 and one in Paris 1834, decided by everything you did.
> - **Pure 16-bit.** SNES-accurate pixel art and an eight-voice score written for the SPC700 sound chip.
>
> *ESRB T / PEGI 12 · PC and Nintendo Switch*

## 2. Vision Statement and Tonal Compass

### 2.1 Vision statement

We are making the JRPG a 1995 Square team might have made after a year in the Bibliothèque nationale: an ensemble epic in which every spell is a piece of music or a stroke of paint, every villain stole credit for the future, and the hero's greatest power is also his greatest danger. It should play with the confidence of *Final Fantasy VI*, think like a good historical novel, and end the way great romances end: with a choice that belongs to both people.

Three sentences every team member should be able to say from memory:

1. **The future is a gift when you name its makers and a theft when you sign it yourself.**
2. **Everything brilliant you do is a risk, and everyone who loves you is a target.**
3. **She chooses her century; he chooses her; both endings are whole.**

## 3. Design Pillars

The five pillars are canonical (CANON §1). Each section turns its pillar into decisions and ends with its test. **A feature that fails its test is redesigned or cut**; disputes go to the Lead Designer with the test written out.

### P1 — The Ensemble Is the Hero

*Every party member has a defining chapter scene, a bond questline, a unique command and a voice in the final Octave. No stat sticks.*

**Design implications.**

| PC | Unique command | Defining story moment | Bond questline | Note in LM-01 / the Octave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PC-01 Min-jun | RECITAL, CONDUCT | CH-13: premieres his *Concerto "Lost Era"* (REP-27); leaves the piano for eight bars to save Lucile | — (the hub; hidden Own Voice) | 1st: D |
| PC-03 Berlioz | ORCHESTRATE | CH-09: leaves his own wedding supper to lead the rescue | BQ-PC03 Idée Fixe | 2nd: E |
| PC-04 Delacroix | CANVAS | CH-09: *Women of Algiers* finale and confession; CH-17: receives the Heart shard (he designs the Door in spring 1834) | BQ-PC04 The Colours of Algiers | 3rd: F♯ |
| PC-05 Alkan | ÉTUDE | CH-15: his tuning forks (KEY-32) let three parties detune the Clefs | BQ-PC05 The Railway Étude | 4th: G |
| PC-06 Chopin | RUBATO | CH-09: *Żal*; the exile who teaches Min-jun to carry home inside music | BQ-PC06 Żal | 5th: A |
| PC-07 Liszt | TRANSCEND → MEPHISTO | CH-13: plays second piano in someone else's concerto | BQ-PC07 Transcendence | 6th: B |
| PC-08 Farrenc | COUNTERPOINT | CH-10: decodes Vane's ledger on the road and finds "L. — to be removed" | BQ-PC08 The Treasury | 7th: C♯, the unresolved leading tone |
| PC-02 Lucile | PLEIN AIR | CH-14: *La Nuit étoilée sur le Panthéon*; "Then teach us the music of the stars." | BQ-PC02 The Hours of Light; BQ-PC02b | 8th: the octave D that completes him |

- **Unique commands do the identity work.** Opus Scores (CANON §11c) let anyone learn shared Harmony skills, so the unique command must stay each character's best tool at every level, and R5 Signatures stay character-locked.
- **The structure forces the spotlight.** Split parties in CH-09 (three threads), CH-13 (three fronts) and CH-15 (three parties) guarantee that every member carries a fight the player cannot skip.
- **Nobody is benched into irrelevance.** Late joiners arrive at max(listed level, party average − 1) (CANON §5); reserves earn 50% EXP, 100% while Farrenc is active.

**This means we do:** write each member a scene only they could carry; give each a boss their command answers (Chopin's Rubato counters BOSS-37's Reverie song); give fighting guests (GST-01–GST-10) one bespoke command each.
**This means we don't:** ship a generic recruit with a borrowed command; write a scene where a member is present but has no line; make a relic that copies another character's unique command; let a single optimal party solve the late game.

**Test.** *Could another character's command or scene do this job?* If yes, redesign it. *Passes:* SYN-23 Les Inadmissibles (Lucile and Farrenc, the two women the law will not seat at the family council) could belong to no other pair. *Would be cut:* a relic that gives anyone Liszt's double-cast.

### P2 — Gift, Not Theft

*Every villain, system and ending asks when carrying the future into the past is a gift and when it is a theft.*

**Design implications.**

- **Credit is a mechanic.** Story performances always name the composer ("*Gaspard de la nuit*, by Maurice Ravel"), and that honesty is what exposes Min-jun to Vane (LAW-20). At optional salons the player chooses the attribution: *name the composer* (Secrecy ×2), *an old teacher's piece* (×1, then −3) or *my own* (×0.5, +1 Shadow, Renown gain ×1.5). Shadow feeds the Shadow Virtuoso (BOSS-31, BOSS-34), never Horizon. The lie disappears from the menu after the Vow (CH-14).
- **Every villain is one kind of theft.** Archon files the industrial future's patents; Vane trades on art-market foreknowledge; Julien replays a Walkman's mixtapes as his own; Delorme signs modern optics and composition; Brücke builds the future in brass. Sœur Marthe (ANA-06) is the positive mirror: boiled water and rehydration given away for no credit.
- **The hero is implicated.** Min-jun gave Lucile an idea, not a canvas, but still changed a life without consent. Delorme ("You gave a girl Monet and you call *me* a thief?", CH-12) and Farrenc (BQ-PC08) say so, and he accepts it.
- **No genius is handed his masterpiece.** Min-jun never gives a real person a documented work and never plays a composer's own future work to that composer (LAW-16, Tone Rule 7). He unlocks doors (Chopin's Ballade block, Delacroix's colour), and they walk through.

**This means we do:** reward naming the makers with story weight; let the most moving scenes be gifts given for nothing (Marthe, the Window coda).
**This means we don't:** let Min-jun patent, profit, prophesy politics or claim a future work in a story beat; write the cabal as cartoon thieves with no case; tell the player that changing history is forbidden (the game asks who has the right, LAW-15).

**Test.** *Does it make the player weigh credit, consent or harm?* *Passes:* the attribution choice. *Would be cut:* a shop that sells "inventions from the future" for francs.

### P3 — Brilliance Has a Price

*Every future act is the strongest option and raises suspicion. The player always chooses between power and cover.*

**Design implications.**

- **The strongest buttons are Anachronic.** Future Recitals carry Heat 1–5 (REP-10 "Scarbo" is Heat 5), Lucile's styles 2–4, ✦ Invocations 3, Improvise 3, every CHRONO action 5 (CANON §11e). With Onlookers on screen, Heat goes straight into the Secrecy Meter.
- **The safe option is real.** Period pieces (REP-31–34) and Min-jun's own works cost nothing. From CH-13 his compositions (REP-27, REP-29, REP-30) join the battle Recital list at Heat 0: becoming a composer is the only power without a price, so the character arc and the economy of risk converge.
- **The decision lives in every layer.** In battle: burst now, or clear the crowd first (a Brass Cue, a Sketch, a crit KO). In dialogue: Bite Your Tongue's three-second choice between the persuasive modern phrasing ("boil the water") and the safe period one. In society: Renown multiplies every gain, so fame makes each slip costlier.
- **Failure moves forward.** The Unmasking (Secrecy 100) is an escape mini-dungeon and a fine, never a game over; losing a Piano Duel changes consequences, never ends the run (CANON §11g).

**This means we do:** keep future options numerically strongest at every tier; show Heat before the choice; make a reckless run valid and exciting.
**This means we don't:** make period options a trap; hide the cost of a choice until after it is made; build stealth fail-states that reload the game; let Secrecy matter once the threat it measures is gone (it is retired in CH-E2).

**Test.** *Does it make the player weigh showing off against hiding?* *Passes:* Onlookers fleeing after 3 turns. *Would be cut:* a late relic that zeroes Heat.

### P4 — Every Rung Is Personal

*The cabal's campaign climbs a visible ladder (CANON §4c). Each rung is carried out by a named member with a motive and strikes at someone Min-jun loves.*

**Design implications.** The sixteen rungs climb five tiers. Every rung names its hand, its motive (M1 exposure in 1833, M2 the portal, SIG the signature) and its victim.

| Tier | Example rung (CANON §4c) | Whose hand | Whom it hurts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watch | 1 The Search (CH-01–02) | Grey Hand; Julien | Min-jun, still a stranger |
| Soft | 2 The Velvet Leash (CH-03–08) | Vane, through Lucile | Lucile and Théo, coerced by a debt |
| Quiet | 8 The Accident (CH-08) | Julien, secretly made lethal by Brücke | Chopin's Pleyel, and Julien's conscience |
| Hard | 9 Abduction (CH-09) | Archon via Roussel | Min-jun's right hand; Berlioz's wedding night |
| Lethal | 14 Cleaning House (CH-14) | Roussel's cleaners | Vane and Lucile, marked for death |

- **Each cabal member gets a face, a scene of understanding and a boss.** Archon's footnote speech (CH-12); Vane reading her early reports: "She stopped being mine long before she stopped writing." (CH-14); Julien's cassette shrine (CH-08).
- **Escalation is legible.** Three spikes are unsanctioned (rungs 5, 8 and part of 10), so the cabal argues with itself on screen and the player can predict who will defect (Julien CH-09, Marthe CH-13, Vane CH-14).

**This means we do:** give every rung a player-facing signal; let the player name culprit and motive within the chapter.
**This means we don't:** use faceless armies as the main threat; escalate with no one choosing to escalate; fight the National Guard, Prefecture or any Seoul civilian (CANON §12).

**Test.** *Can you name who did it, why, and whom it hurt?* *Passes:* the CH-10 Nohant attack (Brücke's automata; M2 plus leverage; Lucile marked). *Would be cut:* an unattributed "mysterious assassin" encounter.

### P5 — Two Cities, One Heart

*Plague-scarred streets against candlelit salons; 1833 Paris against 2026 Seoul. Lucile's answer grows from the whole game, and both epilogues are full playable chapters where the brief's final image lands.*

**Design implications.**

- **Contrast inside Paris.** Gloom and Fog Light States in the quarters and sewers against Candle in the salons; LM-18 *Miasma* against LM-19 *Candlelight Salon*. Cholera is shown through absence (empty chairs, lime pits, orphans, memorial candles) and never as a monster (CANON §16e).
- **Contrast across centuries.** The prologue opens in fluorescent Room B-07; LM-02 carries pentatonic colour into his Paris scenes; CH-15's amalgam zones fuse the two (a subway platform in the ossuary, a convenience store in the Palais-Royal arcade); LM-32 *Eomma* hums under the Seoul amalgam floors.
- **Her answer is grown, not picked.** More than a dozen Horizon inputs, from the CH-06 lesson to the CH-17 final words, are gated by SQ-07 (LAW-23). The Sketchbook's last page (KEY-09) is the only visible gauge.
- **Both endings are whole.** CH-E1 and CH-E2 each run 3.0 h critical path plus 3.0 h optional, with their own areas, bosses, systems and climactic performance of the *Harmonies* finale (CANON §4f). The portrait *Le Frère de demain* lands in both (LAW-26).

**This means we do:** pair every Paris palette with a Seoul counterpart; budget the two epilogues equally.
**This means we don't:** label either ending "true" or "bad"; reduce her answer to a menu pick; ship a cutscene epilogue; render Seoul as neon tourism or Paris as a postcard.

**Test.** *Does it feed the contrast, the bonds or the Horizon?* *Passes:* the Window opening as a rectangle of Seoul's winter light with snow falling upward into a Paris dawn. *Would be cut:* a generic optional dungeon with no tie to either city.

## 4. Player Fantasy and Core Loops

> *"I am the only person in 1833 who has heard the future, and the most dangerous thing I own is my own memory."* (CANON §1)

The fantasy has four verbs. Every system serves at least one of them; a system that serves none is cut.

| Verb | What the player does | Systems (CANON) | Where it pays off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Astonish** | Plays music no one has heard, in salons and in battle | RECITAL (§5c), *Phrase* salons and Renown (§11g), Piano Duels | "Ondine" silences the Lavergne salon (CH-03) |
| **Conceal** | Decides, moment by moment, how much of the future leaks | Secrecy Meter, Onlookers and Heat, Bite Your Tongue, Watchers (§11e) | Reaching CH-14 with the cabal at open war but Paris still unaware |
| **Befriend** | Helps geniuses become themselves, never by handing them answers | Trust Network, bond quests, confessions, Synergies (§11b, §11f) | The Octave of the Lost Era (CH-16) |
| **Choose** | Lives a romance whose ending is computed from the whole game | Horizon, SQ-07 gate, final words (LAW-23) | "Will you come with me?" (CH-17) |

**Three loops.**
- **The turn (2–4 s, CANON §10b):** power or cover. A Heat-4 Recital now, or a Brass Cue to clear the Onlookers first.
- **The chapter (about 2 h):** arrive somewhere new → earn francs and Renown → a friend's problem opens a bond step → a dungeon → a quiet scene (Tone Rule 2) → a set-piece boss → a rung of the ladder lands on someone Min-jun loves.
- **The game (34 h):** interpreter → composer; hiding → trusting; "How do I get home?" → "Where is home?" (CANON §5d).

## 5. Themes

Four themes come from the brief's tonal arc and three from the Director's Addenda. Each lists its question and where the systems, not only the script, carry it.

### 5.1 Artistic legacy
*Who is remembered, and who decides?* Farrenc learns she was forgotten and rediscovered, and fights harder (LAW-18). Min-jun, who has never finished a large work, writes three: the concerto (REP-27), *La Musique des étoiles* (REP-29) and the finale of *Harmonies* (REP-28). In Seoul Lucile leaves her lost canvas anonymous ("It was a gift."); in Paris she signs "L. Aubray" (LAW-21). **Systems:** Own Voice, Shadow, the Canvas Ledger (CH-E2).

### 5.2 Friendship
*What do friends owe each other across two centuries?* Berlioz leaves his own wedding to rescue Min-jun (CH-09). The friends swear the Pact of the Door and keep it for the rest of their lives: the Door is crated in March 1886, and Théo, the sickly boy of 1833, carries it to Seoul at 67 (LAW-22). **Systems:** bond ranks, Ensemble Scenes, keepsakes (KEY-30) that become Remembrances or Lost Era forges.

### 5.3 Trust
*Whom do you tell, and what does it cost them to know?* Confession waits for rank 10, and Archon's Revelation punishes the hesitant (CANON §11f). The Lantern Rule limits even honesty: "tell friends who I am, never when they die" (LAW-18). In Seoul, Brücke chooses to believe Min-jun's promise (LAW-24). **Systems:** the Trust Network, confidant perks, the Hint.

### 5.4 The ethics of changing history
*When is carrying the future a gift?* Bar-lines hold; the ossia, where ordinary people live, changes freely (LAW-15). The game never forbids change; it asks who has the right. In Paris he persuades Rose Lambert to leave no. 12 but cannot stop rue Transnonain (LAW-19), and he swears the Vow (consent, credit, no harm and no profit) only after doing to Lucile, without her consent, what he condemns in the cabal (LAW-20). **Systems:** attribution choice, Shadow, Bite Your Tongue's life-saving modern phrasing.

### 5.5 The romance and its betrayal (A-2)
*Can love that began as a lie become true?* We write **suspense, not surprise**: the player sees the shadowed purse (CH-03) and plays Lucile's own hesitation at Maison Corbel (CH-07) while Min-jun does not know. The sting at CH-09 is personal because her reports read back the player's own Confided lines verbatim (KEY-18). She lies about her errand, never her feelings; stops reporting before the reveal; saves him before she is exposed; pays a price; earns her way back (CANON §16f). She is never a prize: in Paris she proposes. **Systems:** Early Truth, Fracture and Mending, the cracked-heart icon.

### 5.6 The escalating threat (A-1)
*What would people do to keep a door secret?* The cabal fears exposure in 1833 (M1) and, more deeply, Min-jun's return: a living witness to the portal (M2). Brücke has seen it happen: the Kang Disclosure and the Chronometric Survey that hunted travellers (LAW-14). Sixteen rungs climb from watching to killing (CANON §4c). The answer the game gives is pointed: **the cabal was right to fear him and wrong to kill for it.** **Systems:** Secrecy tiers, Watchers, the Hit and Close Call consequences.

### 5.7 The branching ending (A-3)
*Which century is home, and whose choice is it?* Lucile's answer is computed from the SQ-07 gate, the hidden Horizon and his final words (LAW-23). YES: both step into a snowy Seoul afternoon (CH-E1). NO: she asks him to stay, and he throws his recorded goodbye through the Window (CH-E2, LAW-25). He stays because she asks, never by overriding her. Both are full chapters; the portrait lands in both, once as goodbye and once as hello (LAW-26). **Systems:** Window Talks, the Sketchbook signpost, Reverie of the Window.

## 6. Target Audience

**Rating:** ESRB T / PEGI 12 (CANON §16e). **Platforms:** PC and Nintendo Switch. **Languages:** English master; French and Korean localisations.

| Segment | Who | What they want | What serves them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: the SNES generation** | Aged about 30–45; grew up on *FF5*, *FF6*, *Chrono Trigger*; buy retro JRPGs today | Command depth, a soundtrack worth owning, an ensemble that earns its finale, 30+ hours that never pad | Unique commands, Synergies up to the Octave, an SPC700 score, 34 h CP |
| **Secondary: story and romance players** | Aged about 18–35; *Persona*, visual novels, choice-driven RPGs | Characters who change, a romance with consequences, an ending they earned | Bond questlines, Horizon, two playable epilogues |
| **Tertiary: culture lovers** | Classical-music, art-history and Paris enthusiasts, many of them lapsed gamers | Accuracy, beauty, the thrill of meeting Chopin | Real public-domain repertoire, the Almanach in period voice, one difficulty, Rehearsal Mode |
| **Home markets** | Korean and French players | Their own country written with care | A Korean hero never played for exotic colour (CANON §16c); Paris researched to the street and the weekday |

## 7. Comparables: What We Take, What We Avoid

| Comparable | What we take | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| ***Final Fantasy VI*** (1994) | An ensemble with no single lead; magicite → Opus Scores; the opera → CH-13; split parties; a world that opens late (*La Lumière*, CH-14); Blitz → ÉTUDE, Slot → IMPROVISE | Late-game sameness in which everyone learns every spell; an ending the player does not shape |
| ***Final Fantasy V*** (1992) | Command depth; Blue Magic → Delacroix's 36 Studies; the Geomancer's terrain → Lucile's Light States | Blank-slate job characters whose personalities vanish behind the menu |
| ***Chrono Trigger*** (1995) | Dual and triple techs → Duo, Trio, Quartet and Octave Synergies; multiple endings and New Game+ → Reverie of the Window and Da Capo | Free travel between eras; endings collected as a checklist. Our time travel is one door each way |
| ***Persona 3–5*** | Bonds that change the ending; rationed time (Evenings, CANON §11i) | Choosing a partner from a roster; gift-spam; social stats as a grind |
| ***Suikoden II*** (1998) | Political intrigue that is personal; friends on the other side who can turn (Julien, Marthe, Vane) | Recruitment sprawl that thins every character |
| ***Eternal Sonata*** (2007) | Proof that a Chopin-centred JRPG finds an audience; music as the language of battle | A fantasy dreamworld at a composer's deathbed: we never depict real deaths and keep Paris real (CANON §16e) |
| ***Octopath Traveler*** (2018) | Proof that new retro JRPGs sell on Switch and PC | Parallel stories that barely touch; HD-2D presentation (we stay SNES-accurate) |
| ***Pentiment*** (2022) | History lived from below, period voice, art as plot | Its combat-free structure: our ATB battles carry half the game's feeling |
| *Les Misérables* (Hugo, 1862) | Audience recognition of the 1832 barricades; Hugo watches our riot from his window (CH-04) | Confusing our invented 1833 riot (R-08) with his 1832 |

## 8. Game Length

| Act III | CH-10–CH-13 | 17.75–25.75 | 8.00 | 3.50 | Hours 19–26 |

**A chapter is an evening.** The eighteen chapters from CH-P to CH-17 average 1.72 h of critical path and none exceeds 2.5 h, so a player with a two-hour session finishes a chapter, sees its set piece and stops at a natural rest. Act IV is short on critical path and long on optional content: CH-14 opens everything before the continuous night of CH-15–CH-17, and the crypt-door prompt lists unfinished quests before the point of no return (CANON §0c).

## 10. Platform and Technical Target

**Authenticity is a discipline, not a filter.** The constraints below govern what the player sees and hears. There is no cartridge ROM budget (CANON §1), so they never limit how much game there is.

| Constraint (CANON) | Value | What it means for design |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution (§1, §16a) | 256 × 224 | 16 × 14 tiles of 16 px on screen: every room is composed for that window. Text box 240 × 64; 3 lines × 24 characters with a portrait, 4 × 30 without |
| Sprites (R-39, §1) | 32 × 32 cell (figure ≈ 20 × 30 px); 8 sprite palettes of 15 colours plus transparency | Each palette key (CANON §5g) must read in 15 colours; formations share palettes; large bosses go on background layers, as in *FF6*, so the eight Octave members can hold the eight sprite palettes |
| Effects | SNES colour math, HDMA, Mode 7 | Fog, rain and candle glow by colour math; skies by HDMA gradients, so a Light State shows in every frame; Mode 7 for *La Lumière*'s 2:30 finale flight (CANON §4f) |
| Audio (§15a, R-38) | SPC700: 8 voices (music V0–V4, SFX V5–V7), 64 KB audio RAM | The one square/saw synth patch (the Engine, Archon's final forms, the static automata) is the score's only modern timbre, so hearing it is a story event; Grand Mode (8 voices) is kept for five cutscene cues |
| Platforms, text (§1) | PC and Switch, accuracy-first engine; English master; French and Korean | French overflow uses the 4-line box; Korean ships a full font |
| Rating, access (§16e, §11j) | ESRB T / PEGI 12; one difficulty | ATB speed 1–6 and Active/Wait; Rehearsal Mode; text speed 1–5; shape-coded Light State icons |

**Modern mercies we keep, all canon:** Retry battle after a wipe (no money lost), saving anywhere on overworld maps, Rehearsal Mode, and Reverie of the Window instead of a forced replay.

## 11. Unique Selling Points

1. **Your repertoire is your spellbook.** Thirty-seven public-domain works by Debussy, Ravel, Rachmaninoff, Scriabin and Prokofiev (CANON §15d) become three-movement Recitals, Invocations and Synergies, arranged for authentic five-voice SNES music.
2. **Brilliance versus cover.** The strongest move is always the most suspicious one: the Secrecy Meter, Onlookers and Heat turn every battle and salon into a risk decision.
3. **A real city, real geniuses.** Paris 1833, dated to the weekday; more than fifty historical figures written from their letters.
4. **Painting as battlefield control.** Lucile repaints the hour of the day (Light States); Delacroix mixes two elements per stroke and collects 36 enemy Studies.
5. **An eight-note finale.** LM-01's eight notes, one per friend, stop on the leading tone all game and resolve only when Lucile's octave completes them.
6. **A romance that survives a betrayal.** Witty first, tender second; she is never a prize, and in one ending she proposes.
7. **Two complete playable endings.** Seoul 2026 or Paris 1834, computed from the whole game, each with its own areas, bosses, systems and performance of the *Harmonies* finale.
8. **A Korean hero in Romantic-era Europe,** written with care: a perspective JRPGs have almost never offered.

## 12. What This Game Is Not

- **Not a rhythm game.** Timing inputs exist; Rehearsal Mode makes them perfect, and no story beat requires rhythm skill.
- **Not a dating sim.** One heroine, no roster, no gift-spam; she decides her answer.
- **Not a time-hopping adventure.** One door in, one window out. The player never travels between eras at will.
- **Not alternate history.** Bar-lines hold: Chopin will die in 1849, the April 1834 insurrection happens, Impressionism still arrives in the 1860s–70s (LAW-15).
- **Not a genius-fixing fantasy.** Min-jun never hands a real person their documented masterpiece (LAW-16).
- **Not a fantasy world in period costume.** No wizards, demons or gods; the supernatural is acoustic (Tone Rule 5).
- **Not grimdark.** T rating, no gore, no corpses, no lecture past three boxes (Tone Rules 3, 8).
- **Not "retro-inspired".** Not HD-2D, not high-resolution pixel art: SNES rules.
- **Not a game with a true ending.** Neither branch is good or bad, and neither is canon over the other.
