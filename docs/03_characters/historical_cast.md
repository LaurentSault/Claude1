## 1. Conventions

**Header line** (italic): dates · age in 1833 (CANON §7; "→" = birthday inside 5 Mar – 22 Dec 1833) · real 1833 whereabouts · in-game role · first chapter · liberty flag · portrait (tier, name tab, bespoke expression). Party members live in party.md, cabal members in anachronists.md.

**Biographies** state only the record for 1830–34; anything invented is labelled as a scene. **Scene lists** bind the scenario, quest and world docs (CANON §0b): what each figure does, wants from Min-jun and gives him.

**Portraits** (CANON §16h). Tier B (48 × 48; neutral, smile, grave plus the bespoke expression of dialogue_style_guide §3.4): NPC-01–30, NPC-33, FIC-02–05, 07–09, 12–14, 18–19. Tier A: FIC-01. Others Tier C: `[N:Name]`, 4 × 30 box. **Sprites:** 32 × 32 cells, ~20 × 30 px figure (R-39).

**Sample lines** are original game dialogue in the CANON §16a format, one box each; none is or imitates a documented quotation (dialogue_style_guide §4). *(canon)* marks lines lifted from CANON. Minor Tier C originals get one signature line; overworld_and_towns.md carries their barks.

**Binding staging rules.**
- **Sand** never shares a scene, line or battle with Liszt, Delacroix or Chopin before CH-E2 ends; **"Florestan"** is never introduced by name to Mendelssohn, Chopin or Liszt; **Clara Wieck** is never romanticised (§16e, R-56).
- **No NPC learns the future** (Tone Rule 7). Min-jun knows famous death dates (Nourrit 1839, Paganini 1840, Musset 1857) and never tells (LAW-18): at most a silent `{EMOTE:PC-01:...}`. Telling a non-party NPC is a Reckless Confession (Secrecy +25, nothing gained).
- Society says "Monsieur Kang"; "Monsieur Min-jun" is corrected kindly (§16c).
- Each NPC's **Almanach** entry unlocks at their first scene, in period voice, holding nothing later than that scene's date.

## 2. Musicians and the Stage

#### NPC-17 · Luigi Cherubini
*14 Sep 1760 – 15 Mar 1842 · 72 → 73 · Director of the Conservatoire, rue Bergère, since 1822 · Throws Min-jun out; grudging respect; admits Offenbach · CH-02 · Liberty N · B "Cherubini" (`scowl`)*

- **1830–34.** Florentine master of French opera and sacred music (*Médée*, 1797; the C-minor Requiem, 1816) who runs the Conservatoire by the rule book: in 1823 he turned away the twelve-year-old Liszt because foreigners were barred, and Berlioz has feuded with him since the 1820s. His last opera, *Ali-Baba*, opens in July 1833 and fails; in November he admits the foreign boy Jacob Offenbach.
- **Scenes.** **CH-02:** refuses Min-jun an audition at the railings (R-05). **CH-03:** after BOSS-04 the choir boys sleep through the night, and word comes through the porter that "the Corean" is not to be thrown out again. **CH-12 (Sat 30 Nov):** Min-jun watches him bend for Offenbach the rule he once broke Liszt on. **CH-14:** one nod at Hiller's concert. *Wants:* clean counterpoint. *Gives:* nothing he admits, and proof that Paris can still make room for a foreigner.
- **Voice.** Curt, Italianate, one clause per verdict; praise disguised as insult (§16b).
```
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:scowl] A foreigner, no papers, no teacher. The rule is the rule. Go.
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:grave] The boys slept last night. I am told nothing. I prefer it.
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:scowl] I refused a boy from Hungary once. He forgave me. I did not.
```
- **Sprite.** Small, upright, white hair combed forward, black coat, rosette, cane. **Ties.** Berlioz bristles (comedy, never cruelty); Alkan, a prize-winning former student, is his quiet pride; no contact with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-19 · Harriet Smithson
*18 Mar 1800 – 3 Mar 1854 · 32 → 33 · Paris; leg broken 16 Mar · Berlioz's bride · CH-03 · Liberty N · B "Miss Smithson" (`radiant`)*

- **1830–34.** The Irish actress whose Ophelia and Juliet conquered Paris in 1827 and became Berlioz's *idée fixe*; after his concert of 9 Dec 1832 she agreed to meet him. She broke her leg stepping from a carriage on 16 Mar 1833; with debts mounting, a benefit was given for her at the Théâtre-Italien on Tue 2 Apr. She marries Berlioz at the British Embassy on Thu 3 Oct, Liszt a witness. Her French is halting; his English is worse.
- **Scenes.** **CH-02:** tavern gossip. **CH-03:** the benefit seen from the gallery; an optional Evening at her lodging, where she finds the foreigner speaks English and has him translate Hector's latest letter. **CH-09:** the wedding (LOC-P23); when Petit-Louis brings the Bercy address, she sends Berlioz after his friend. **CH-E2:** in the hall on 22 Dec. *Wants:* someone to talk to in English. *Gives:* the rescue's blessing; in BQ-PC03, the truth about the man behind the *idée fixe*.
- **Voice.** Warm, frank, wry. English is a private channel, a Slip (Secrecy +5) before Onlookers.
```
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:smile] [in English] English! You talk like a sailor, but bless you, it's English.
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:grave] Ophelia filled theatres. Harriet fills a sickroom. Sit down.
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:radiant] Go after your friend, Hector. I married you for that heart.
```
- **Sprite.** Fair ringlets, ivory gown; a crutch in CH-03, white roses in CH-09. **Ties.** Berlioz, Liszt; Lucile sketches her at the supper; her wedding night is the cabal's abduction night. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-42 · Henri Herz
*6 Jan 1803 – 5 Jan 1888 · 30 · Paris · Salon cutting contest (BOSS-03) · CH-03 · Liberty Y (minor: the contest) · C*

- **1830–34.** Vienna-born, Paris-trained, the best-paid salon pianist of the day, whose operatic variations sell enormously and whom Chopin's admirers mock.
- **Scenes.** **CH-03 (Sat 27 Apr):** at Mme Lavergne's, a three-Passage "friendly contest" that teaches the duel wheel and cannot be lost badly; after "Ondine" he is the first to applaud. **CH-05+:** a rival on T2 programmes. *Gives:* the tutorial, and gossip that brings T3 invitations.
- **Voice.** Breezy professional; fees, encores, flattery.
```
[N:Henri Herz] Three passages, monsieur, all in friendship. I never lose a friendly contest.
[N:Henri Herz] Variations on any theme you like. Name it, and I'll have six by supper.
[N:Henri Herz] "Ondine"? Never heard of the lady. Who is her publisher?
```
- **Sprite.** Curled hair, lavender waistcoat, jewelled pin, a pose at the keyboard. **Ties.** Min-jun's first rival; Lucile sells fans in the card room that night; Vane watches the contest. Hook: T2 rival programmes. **Liberty.** Y (R-10): salon contests were common; this one is invented.

#### NPC-29 · Ferdinand Hiller
*24 Oct 1811 – 11 May 1885 · 21 → 22 · Paris · Chopin's and Liszt's friend; the 15 Dec Bach concert · CH-05 · Liberty N · B "Hiller" (`cheerful`)*

- **1830–34.** Frankfurt pianist and composer, Hummel's pupil, in Paris since 1828; close to Chopin, Liszt, Berlioz and Mendelssohn. On Sun 15 Dec 1833 he, Chopin and Liszt play Bach's concerto for three keyboards.
- **Scenes.** **CH-05:** the friend who makes introductions at Pleyel's. **CH-07+:** host of SYN-09's Ensemble Scene (Liszt and Chopin at his rooms). **CH-14:** invites the party to LOC-P35, the quiet scene before the Hôtel de Vane. *Gives:* tickets and the most generous ear in Paris.
- **Voice.** Cheerful connector; names, dates, invitations.
```
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:cheerful] Bach, three keyboards! Chopin wants the quiet part. Liszt, the rest.
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:smile] Frankfurt sends you its regards. Frankfurt has no idea who you are.
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:grave] Sit near the front. Some evenings deserve to be remembered.
```
- **Sprite.** Round face, dark curls, a pocket stuffed with letters. **Ties.** Chopin and Liszt; no contact with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-45 · Marie Moke-Pleyel
*4 Sep 1811 (some sources 4 Jul) – 30 Mar 1875 · 21 → 22 · Paris, pianist · Berlioz's ex-fiancée: the comic-awkward scene · CH-05 · Liberty N · C "Mme Pleyel"*

- **1830–34.** A virtuoso pianist engaged to Berlioz in 1830; while he was in Rome her mother broke it off, and in 1831 she married Camille Pleyel. Berlioz's own account of the revenge he plotted and abandoned on the road is famous.
- **Scenes.** **CH-05:** in Pleyel's showroom she and Berlioz are exquisitely polite, Camille notices nothing, and Min-jun is left holding the conversation (Berlioz mutters about pistols and a lady's-maid disguise).
- **Voice.** Bright, cutting, entirely composed.
```
[N:Mme Pleyel] Monsieur Berlioz. You look well. Married soon, they say. How brave of her.
[N:Mme Pleyel] Kalkbrenner taught me, Moscheles taught me. Nobody taught me to be quiet.
[N:Mme Pleyel] Try the treble, Monsieur Kang. My husband's pianos forgive nothing.
```
- **Sprite.** Dark hair in high loops, emerald silk. **Ties.** Berlioz and Pleyel; none with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N; nothing beyond the documented story is implied.

#### NPC-46 · Auguste Franchomme
*10 Apr 1808 – 21 Jan 1884 · 24 → 25 · Paris, cellist; Touraine with Chopin in Aug 1833 · Takes Chopin to Touraine; Offenbach's idol · CH-06 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The finest young cellist in Paris and Chopin's closest musical friend; their *Grand Duo* on themes from *Robert le diable* is published in July 1833, and in August they stay at Le Côteau with the Forest family.
- **Scenes.** **CH-06:** at Chopin's with the *Grand Duo* proofs (flavour item); on Sun 11 Aug he carries Chopin off to Touraine (R-57). **CH-13:** Offenbach spots him and nearly loses his place.
- **Voice.** Easy, affectionate, unhurried.
```
[N:Franchomme] Touraine, Frédéric. Fresh air, the Forests' cellar, and no pupils for a month.
[N:Franchomme] A cello says what a pianist only hints. I've told him so for a year.
[N:Franchomme] The boy from Cologne follows me about. Tell him to practise instead.
```
- **Sprite.** Tall, fair moustache, cello case on his back. **Ties.** Chopin and Offenbach; none with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N (the compressed summer is R-57).

#### NPC-02 · Sigismond Thalberg
*8 Jan 1812 – 27 Apr 1871 · 21 · Vienna; German tours · Duel rival (BOSS-08), later GST-10; private returns Dec 1833 and Feb 1834 · CH-07 · Liberty Y · B "Thalberg" (`intent`)*

- **1830–34.** Born near Geneva and raised in Vienna in the household of Prince Moritz Dietrichstein, generally taken to be his father; Hummel's pupil, touring the German lands since 1830 with operatic fantasies of fearsome polish. His Paris debut lies in 1835–36, his duel with Liszt in 1837.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07 (Sat 7 Sep):** the embassy's private guest; Vane sponsors the duel and Brücke rigs Min-jun's action (BOSS-08). Win or lose, he tries the rigged instrument afterwards and apologises: the first outsider to see that someone wants Min-jun humiliated. **CH-14 (SQ-20):** Belgiojoso's guest; BOSS-35; GST-10. **CH-E2 (Feb 1834):** the rematch, with only REP-27–34 and *Harmonies* (LAW-25). *Wants:* a fair contest. *Gives:* respect and a guest slot.
- **Voice.** Impeccably courteous, short formal sentences, competitive beneath; never gloats, never cheats.
```
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:intent] His Excellency's floor, your music, my hands. Begin when you like.
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:grave] Your treble was stiff as wire. Not your doing. I shall learn whose.
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:smile] The princess has two pianos and no patience. Again, monsieur?
```
- **Sprite.** Tall, immaculate, dove-grey coat, hands still; duel idle a slow wrist roll. **Ties.** Vane's unwitting instrument, Liszt's future rival; no contact with Lucile. **Liberty.** Y (R-10): the private visits and the early three-hand device; his Viennese ties make an embassy visit natural, and Count Rudolf Apponyi keeps it out of his journal.

#### NPC-33 · Gioachino Rossini
*29 Feb 1792 – 13 Nov 1868 · 41 · Paris · Guest of honour at the embassy; judges the Thalberg duel · CH-07 · Liberty N · B "Rossini" (`savouring`)*

- **1830–34.** Since *Guillaume Tell* (1829) he has written no opera; he lives in Paris, litigating over the pension the July Revolution put in doubt, famous, idle by choice, a celebrated gourmand.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07:** judges the duel; his verdict scene changes with the result but praises both (Renown either way). **CH-13 (optional):** in a box, eating sorbet through his own overture.
- **Voice.** Lazily witty; food as metaphor; generous verdicts.
```
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:savouring] A duel needs a judge and a supper. Tonight I am both.
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:smile] Thalberg was perfect. You were impossible. I prefer impossible.
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:savouring] Never trust a gala without sorbet in the second part.
```
- **Sprite.** Stout, chestnut wig, napkin tucked into his cravat, tasting from a spoon. **Ties.** Judges Min-jun, unaware of Vane's rigging; no Lucile contact. **Liberty.** N (the duel is R-10).

#### NPC-48 · Anna Liszt
*28 May 1788 – 6 Feb 1866 · 44 → 45 · Paris, rue de Provence (no. 61) · Liszt's mother, warm and practical · CH-07 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Born Anna Lager in Krems and widowed in 1827 when Adam Liszt died at Boulogne, she keeps house for her son and keeps him fed.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07+:** the flat is an optional rest (soup; a free inn-style rest once per chapter). **CH-14:** she mends the worn white glove Liszt will give at the Window (KEY-30).
- **Voice.** Practical Austrian warmth; scolds with soup.
```
[N:Anna Liszt] He eats nothing before he plays and everything after. Sit. Eat now.
[N:Anna Liszt] A virtuoso with a hole in his glove? Not while I have thread.
[N:Anna Liszt] [in German] Krems is a small town. Paris is a small town with more noise.
```
- **Sprite.** Plump, white cap, grey shawl, darning in her lap. **Ties.** Liszt; feeds Lucile too in CH-14; no cabal contact. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-03 · Jacques Offenbach
*20 Jun 1819 – 5 Oct 1880 · 13 → 14 · Cologne; Paris from Nov; admitted 30 Nov · Comic guest (GST-08) · CH-12 · Liberty Y (minor: the Opéra pit) · B "Offenbach" (`cheeky`)*

- **1830–34.** Jacob, son of the Cologne cantor Isaac Offenbach, is brought to Paris with his brother Julius in November 1833; Cherubini waives the rule against foreigners after hearing him and admits him to the cello class on Sat 30 Nov.
- **Scenes.** **CH-12:** queuing at the gate, then admitted. **CH-13:** a substitute cellist in the Opéra pit who follows Chopin into the corridors "for the adventure" (GST-08 GALOP, BOSS-19). **CH-E2:** guest again. Comedy never touches grief scenes; his faith is respected.
- **Voice.** Cheeky, quick, self-mocking.
```
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:cheeky] Substitute cellist. Substitute for whom? Whoever's ill tonight!
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:smile] Monsieur Chopin walks like a duke. I walk like a duck. We both arrive.
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:cheeky] Franchomme nodded at me. Once! I shall live on it till spring.
```
- **Sprite.** All elbows, oversized coat, a cello case taller than his shoulders. **Ties.** Chopin, Farrenc, Cherubini; fights Roussel (BOSS-19); Lucile's ally in CH-E2. **Liberty.** Y (R-25): the Opéra hires a substitute for a cellist down with winter fever, on Franchomme's word.

#### NPC-35 · François-Antoine Habeneck
*22 Jan 1781 – 8 Feb 1849 · 52 · Opéra conductor · Yields his podium to Berlioz, sulking · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Principal conductor of the Opéra and founder (1828) of the Société des Concerts du Conservatoire, which taught Paris Beethoven's symphonies; he conducted the *Fantastique* premiere.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** Archon insists on Berlioz (R-29); Habeneck surrenders the podium and sulks in the wings. **CH-14:** conducts Hiller's concert.
- **Voice.** Gruff pride; tempo is law.
```
[N:Habeneck] I gave Paris its Beethoven. Tonight I give Berlioz my podium. One evening.
[N:Habeneck] Watch the bow, gentlemen, not the pianists. Pianists lie about tempo.
[N:Habeneck] Three keyboards and one orchestra. Monsieur Hiller, after you.
```
- **Sprite.** Heavy brows, a violin bow for a baton, snuffbox. **Ties.** Berlioz; displaced by the Baron's whim, never knowing why. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-34 · Giacomo Meyerbeer
*5 Sep 1791 – 2 May 1864 · 41 → 42 · Paris · Gala guest · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Master of the Opéra since *Robert le diable* (1831), anxious, courteous and endlessly revising his next grand opera.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** in a box near the King's; asks Berlioz how the brass was balanced while Min-jun bites back a century of orchestration (a `[SLIP]` prompt).
- **Voice.** Anxious courtesy; forever revising.
```
[N:Meyerbeer] Robert runs a third season and I still rewrite the fifth act.
[N:Meyerbeer] A concerto from Corée, Monsieur Kang. Paris will want another by spring.
[N:Meyerbeer] Your brass, Monsieur Berlioz, is too loud. Teach me how.
```
- **Sprite.** Slight, spectacles, a programme rolled tight. **Ties.** Berlioz and Nourrit; blind to the Baron's plot. **Liberty.** N; his Judaism is respected.

#### NPC-38 · Adolphe Nourrit
*3 Mar 1802 – 8 Mar 1839 · 31 · Paris Opéra · Gala tenor · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The Opéra's leading tenor, creator of Arnold in *Guillaume Tell* and Robert in *Robert le diable*, a liberal who sang patriotic songs from the stage after the July days.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** sings in Part One of the gala (KEY-25) and gives Min-jun a singer's advice backstage. Min-jun knows how his life ends and says nothing.
- **Voice.** A singer's warmth; thinks in breaths.
```
[N:Nourrit] I sang Robert two years running. Tonight I sing for orphans. Better.
[N:Nourrit] Breathe before the phrase, monsieur, not inside it. Pianists forget.
[N:Nourrit] Your concerto has no singer. Lend me its slow theme, afterwards.
```
- **Sprite.** Handsome, costume doublet under a greatcoat. **Ties.** Min-jun; unaware of the rafters fight. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-37 · Marie Taglioni
*23 Apr 1804 – 22 Apr 1884 · 28 → 29 · Paris Opéra · Her *Sylphide* interlude covers the rafters fight · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The ballerina who made *La Sylphide* (March 1832) the sensation of Paris, rising on pointe and flying on the Opéra's wires.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** her Part Two scene plays below while the rafters team (DE, LU, AL) fights among the very wires that carry her; afterwards she thanks them for keeping off them.
- **Voice.** Cool, precise, a worker's pride.
```
[N:Mlle Taglioni] The wires take me at nine. Whatever fights up there, keep off them.
[N:Mlle Taglioni] A sylph lands without a sound. You landed like a piano.
[N:Mlle Taglioni] One flight is luck. Two are work. I fly again tomorrow.
```
- **Sprite.** White tulle and wings; a 3-frame float loop. **Ties.** The rafters team (DE, LU, AL); unaware of Brücke. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-39 · Narcisse Girard

#### NPC-22 · Niccolò Paganini
*27 Oct 1782 – 27 May 1840 · 50 → 51 · Touring; Paris in Dec · Legend in talk; attends the 22 Dec concert · CH-E2 · Liberty N · B "Paganini" (`piercing`)*

- **1830–34.** The violinist whose Paris debut (March 1831) and Liszt's hearing of him (April 1832) changed what virtuosity meant. He attends Berlioz's concert of 22 Dec 1833 and in January 1834 asks him for a work for his Stradivari viola, the seed of *Harold en Italie*.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07–CH-14:** a legend in talk; BOSS-36 is an Echo of the legend, not the man. **CH-E2:** the side-box stare at Min-jun; in January Berlioz receives his note (KEY-37). *Gives:* the commission.
- **Voice.** Few words, each a verdict.
```
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:piercing] You. The pianist from nowhere. You listen like a thief.
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:grave] A viola, a Stradivari, and nothing to play on it. Write it.
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:neutral] Monsieur Berlioz. Your symphony. Again, and louder.
```
- **Sprite.** Gaunt, long black hair, loose black coat, utterly still. **Ties.** Liszt (BQ-PC07), Berlioz; no contact with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N; the 1838 kneeling is never shown (R-53).

#### NPC-01 · César Franck
*10 Dec 1822 – 8 Nov 1890 · 10 → 11 · Liège · Prodigy on the Rhine road · CH-11 · Liberty N · B "César Franck" (`shy`)*

- **1830–34.** A pupil of the Liège music school since about 1830, whose father will tour him in Belgium in 1834–35 and move the family to Paris in 1835 (R-22). He never appears in Paris in this game.
- **Scenes.** **CH-11:** plays nightly on the Hôtel de la Meuse's salon piano. Chopin, who joins the tour here and was a pushed prodigy himself, asks the father to let the boy stop: a Chopin bond scene (value set by secrecy_and_trust). Min-jun knows what César will become and says nothing.
- **Voice.** Shy, polite, longing to stop.
```
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:shy] Monsieur Chopin, will you tell Papa that I may stop now?
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:smile] When I'm grown I want an instrument with more keys. Many more.
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:shy] Papa says the Hummel is for gentlemen. Are you gentlemen?
```
- **Sprite.** Small, round-faced, a Sunday collar too stiff. **Ties.** Chopin; no contact with Lucile or the cabal. **Liberty.** N.

## 3. Painters and Sculptors

#### NPC-09 · J.-A.-D. Ingres
*29 Aug 1780 – 14 Jan 1867 · 52 → 53 · Paris (*Portrait of M. Bertin*, 1832) · Academic foe of Lucile's art; the hostile juror (BOSS-32 at Favor −30) · CH-06 · Liberty N · B "Ingres" (`disdain`)*

- **1830–34.** Head of the classical school, of the Institut since 1825; *M. Bertin* hangs in the Salon of 1833, and *The Martyrdom of Saint Symphorian*, for 1834, will meet a cool reception that sends him back to Rome.
- **Scenes.** **CH-06:** passing Corbel's window on a Tuesday, he gives *Le Pont Royal* its verdict; at the Louvre he orders Chassériau back to his Raphael. **CH-E2 (Sat 15 Feb):** his verdict opens BOSS-32; on 1 Mar his *Saint Symphorian* hangs on the line while her canvas is skied. He despises Delorme's lens tricks as much as Lucile's fog, so his hostility is principle, never the cabal's. *Gives:* an honest antagonist.
- **Voice.** Verdicts, not conversation; his condescension to women is shown, never endorsed.
```
INGRES  [P:NPC-09:disdain] Weather, not painting.
INGRES  [P:NPC-09:grave] Théodore. Your easel. Copy the Raphael, not the young lady.
INGRES  [P:NPC-09:disdain] Fog has no anatomy, mademoiselle. Draw first. Then we talk.
```
- *(Line 1 is canon.)* **Sprite.** Stocky, heavy brows, black coat, arms folded. **Ties.** Delacroix's rival; Chassériau's master; Lucile's foe, never the cabal's. **Liberty.** N.
