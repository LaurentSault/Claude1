## 1. Conventions

**Entry format.** An italic header line: dates · age in 1833 (CANON §7; "→" = birthday inside 5 Mar – 22 Dec 1833) · real 1833 whereabouts · in-game role · first chapter · liberty flag · portrait (tier, name tab, bespoke expression). Then **1830–34** (the record only; anything invented is a scene), **Scenes** by chapter, binding on the scenario, quest and world docs (CANON §0b), one **Wants / Gives / Hook** line (what the figure wants of Min-jun, gives him, and which quest or system it feeds), **Voice** with three sample lines, and **Sprite · Ties · Liberty**. Party members live in party.md, cabal members in anachronists.md.

**Portraits** (CANON §16h). Tier B (48 × 48; neutral, smile, grave plus the bespoke expression of dialogue_style_guide §3.4): NPC-01–30, NPC-33, FIC-02–05, 07–09, 12–14, 18–19. Tier A: FIC-01. Others Tier C: `[N:Name]`, 4 × 30 box. **Sprites:** 32 × 32 cells, ~20 × 30 px figure (R-39).

**Sample lines** are original game dialogue in the CANON §16a format, one box each; none is or imitates a documented quotation (dialogue_style_guide §4). *(canon)* marks lines lifted from CANON. Townsfolk barks live in overworld_and_towns.md.

**Binding staging rules.**
- **Sand** never shares a scene, line or battle with Liszt, Delacroix or Chopin before CH-E2 ends; **"Florestan"** is never introduced by name to Mendelssohn, Chopin or Liszt; **Clara Wieck** is never romanticised (§16e, R-56).
- **No NPC learns the future** (Tone Rule 7). Min-jun knows famous death dates (Nourrit 1839, Paganini 1840, Musset 1857) and never tells (LAW-18): at most a silent `{EMOTE:PC-01:...}`.
- **Reckless Confession:** only the NPCs listed in secrecy_and_trust §4.6, once each (+25). Here: Mère Gaudin, Petit-Louis, Corbel, Heine and Sand; each entry notes the reaction.
- Society says "Monsieur Kang"; "Monsieur Min-jun" is corrected kindly (§16c).
- Each NPC's **Almanach** entry unlocks at their first scene, in period voice, holding nothing later than that scene's date.

## 2. Musicians and the Stage

#### NPC-17 · Luigi Cherubini
*14 Sep 1760 – 15 Mar 1842 · 72 → 73 · Director of the Conservatoire, rue Bergère, since 1822 · Throws Min-jun out; grudging respect; admits Offenbach · CH-02 · Liberty N · B "Cherubini" (`scowl`)*

- **1830–34.** Florentine master of opera and sacred music (*Médée*, 1797; the C-minor Requiem, 1816) who runs the Conservatoire by the rule book: in 1823 he turned the boy Liszt away as a foreigner, and he has feuded with Berlioz since the 1820s. *Ali-Baba* (July 1833) fails.
- **Scenes.** **CH-02:** refuses Min-jun an audition at the railings (R-05). **CH-03:** after BOSS-04 the choir boys sleep through the night, and the porter is told "the Corean" is not to be thrown out again. **CH-12 (Sat 30 Nov):** bends for Offenbach the rule he once broke Liszt on. **CH-14:** a nod at Hiller's concert.
- *Wants:* clean counterpoint and a quiet building. *Gives:* nothing he admits, and proof that Paris can make room for a foreigner. *Hook:* BOSS-04 in his undercroft.
- **Voice.** Curt, Italianate; praise disguised as insult (§16b).
```
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:scowl] A foreigner, no papers, no teacher. The rule is the rule. Go.
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:grave] The boys slept last night. I am told nothing. I prefer it.
CHERUBINI  [P:NPC-17:scowl] I refused a boy from Hungary once. He forgave me. I did not.
```
- **Sprite.** Small, upright, white hair combed forward, black coat, rosette, cane. **Ties.** Berlioz bristles (comedy, never cruelty); Alkan, a prize-winning former pupil, is his quiet pride. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-19 · Harriet Smithson
*18 Mar 1800 – 3 Mar 1854 · 32 → 33 · Paris; leg broken 16 Mar · Berlioz's bride · CH-03 · Liberty N · B "Miss Smithson", "Mme Berlioz" from CH-09 (`radiant`)*

- **1830–34.** The Irish actress whose Ophelia and Juliet conquered Paris in 1827 and became Berlioz's *idée fixe*; she agreed to meet him after his concert of 9 Dec 1832. Her fall and her debts bring a benefit at the Théâtre-Italien (Tue 2 Apr). Her French is halting; his English is worse.
- **Scenes.** **CH-02:** tavern gossip. **CH-03:** the benefit from the gallery; an optional Evening at her lodging, where she finds the foreigner speaks English and has him translate Hector's latest letter. **CH-09 (LOC-P23):** Petit-Louis taps at the supper-room window; Hector begins to ask and she tells him to go before he has finished; he leaves the menu note on his chair (lore §13.14), and she reads it aloud and has the cake guarded. **CH-E2:** in the hall on 22 Dec.
- *Wants:* someone to talk to in English. *Gives:* the rescue's blessing, and in BQ-PC03 the truth about the man behind the *idée fixe*. *Hook:* BQ-PC03; the CH-03 letter Evening.
- **Voice.** Warm, frank, wry. English is a private channel, a Slip (Secrecy +5) before Onlookers.
```
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:smile] [in English] English! You talk like a sailor, but bless you, it's English.
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:grave] Ophelia filled theatres. Harriet fills a sickroom. Sit down.
HARRIET  [P:NPC-19:radiant] He left me a note on the menu. Four hours married. I'm keeping it.
```
- **Sprite.** Fair ringlets, ivory gown; a crutch in CH-03, white roses in CH-09. **Ties.** Liszt, her witness; her wedding night is the cabal's abduction night; Lucile paints her a fan of white roses as a belated wedding gift (CH-12+). **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-42 · Henri Herz
*6 Jan 1803 – 5 Jan 1888 · 30 · Paris · Salon cutting contest (BOSS-03) · CH-03 · Liberty Y (minor: the contest) · C*

- **1830–34.** Vienna-born, Paris-trained, the best-paid salon pianist of the day, whose operatic variations sell enormously and whom Chopin's admirers mock.
- **Scenes.** **CH-03 (Sat 27 Apr):** at Mme Lavergne's, a three-Passage "friendly contest" that teaches the duel wheel and cannot be lost badly; after "Ondine" he is the first to applaud. **CH-05–CH-14:** a rival on T2 programmes; Vane books him for Lavergne's nights as cover when masked guests attend (Cabal Presence).
- *Wants:* the encore and the fee. *Gives:* the duel tutorial, and gossip that brings T3 invitations. *Hook:* T2 rival programmes.
- **Voice.** Breezy professional; fees, encores, flattery.
```
[N:Henri Herz] Three passages, monsieur, all in friendship. I never lose a friendly contest.
[N:Henri Herz] Variations on any theme you like. Name it, and I'll have six by supper.
[N:Henri Herz] “Ondine”? Never heard of the lady. Who is her publisher?
```
- **Sprite.** Curled hair, lavender waistcoat, jewelled pin, a pose at the keyboard. **Ties.** Min-jun's first rival. **Liberty.** Y (R-10): salon contests were common; this one is invented.

#### NPC-29 · Ferdinand Hiller
*24 Oct 1811 – 11 May 1885 · 21 → 22 · Paris · Chopin's and Liszt's friend; the 15 Dec Bach concert · CH-05 · Liberty N · B "Hiller" (`cheerful`)*

- **1830–34.** Frankfurt pianist and composer, Hummel's pupil, in Paris since 1828; close to Chopin, Liszt, Berlioz and Mendelssohn.
- **Scenes.** **CH-05:** makes introductions at Pleyel's. **CH-07 (after the Sat 7 Sep duel)+:** hosts SYN-09's Ensemble Scene, Liszt and Chopin at his rooms. **CH-14 (Sun 15 Dec):** invites the party to LOC-P35 for Bach's three-keyboard concerto (Habeneck conducting): the quiet scene after the Hôtel de Vane (Mon 9 Dec) and Sand's departure (Thu 12 Dec), before the Night of Stars (Wed 18 Dec).
- *Wants:* his friends at one keyboard, or three. *Gives:* tickets and the most generous ear in Paris. *Hook:* SYN-09; Liszt's concert BP (secrecy_and_trust §3.4).
- **Voice.** Cheerful connector; names, dates, invitations.
```
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:cheerful] Bach, three keyboards! Chopin wants the quiet part. Liszt, the rest.
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:smile] Frankfurt sends you its regards. Frankfurt has no idea who you are.
HILLER  [P:NPC-29:grave] Sit near the front. Some evenings deserve to be remembered.
```
- **Sprite.** Round face, dark curls, a pocket stuffed with letters. **Ties.** Buys one of Lucile's fans in CH-05 for his mother in Frankfurt, the first of her fans to leave France. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-45 · Marie Moke-Pleyel
*4 Sep 1811 (some sources 4 Jul) – 30 Mar 1875 · 21 → 22 · Paris, pianist · Berlioz's ex-fiancée: the comic-awkward scene · CH-05 · Liberty N · C "Mme Pleyel"*

- **1830–34.** A virtuoso pianist engaged to Berlioz in 1830; while he was in Rome her mother broke it off, and in 1831 she married Camille Pleyel. Berlioz's own account of the revenge he plotted and abandoned is famous.
- **Scenes.** **CH-05:** in Pleyel's showroom she and Berlioz are exquisitely polite, Camille notices nothing, and Min-jun holds the conversation while Berlioz mutters about pistols and a lady's-maid disguise.
- *Wants:* to be the best pianist in the room. *Gives:* the dare that makes Min-jun play *Jeux d'eau* (REP-11) in the showroom. *Hook:* Berlioz's CH-05 BP beat (secrecy_and_trust §3.4).
- **Voice.** Bright, cutting, entirely composed.
```
[N:Mme Pleyel] Monsieur Berlioz. You look well. Married soon, they say. How brave of her.
[N:Mme Pleyel] Kalkbrenner taught me, Moscheles taught me. Nobody taught me to be quiet.
[N:Mme Pleyel] Try the treble, Monsieur Kang. My husband's pianos forgive nothing.
```
- **Sprite.** Dark hair in high loops, apricot silk. **Liberty.** N; nothing beyond the documented story is implied.

#### NPC-46 · Auguste Franchomme
*10 Apr 1808 – 21 Jan 1884 · 24 → 25 · Paris, cellist; Touraine with Chopin in Aug 1833 · Takes Chopin to Touraine; Offenbach's idol · CH-06 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The finest young cellist in Paris and Chopin's closest musical friend; their *Grand Duo* on themes from *Robert le diable* is published in July 1833, and in August they stay at Le Côteau with the Forest family.
- **Scenes.** **CH-06:** at Chopin's with the *Grand Duo* proofs; on Sun 11 Aug, just after the chapter, he carries Chopin off to Touraine (R-57). **CH-13:** at the gala. His word got the newly arrived Offenbach into the pit for a cellist down with winter fever (R-25); the boy spots him and nearly loses his place.
- *Wants:* Chopin out of Paris for a month. *Gives:* the *Grand Duo* flavour item; vouches Offenbach into the pit. *Hook:* Offenbach's CH-E2 errand for his signature on the boy's gala programme.
- **Voice.** Easy, affectionate, unhurried.
```
[N:Franchomme] Touraine, Frédéric. Fresh air, the Forests' cellar, and no pupils for a month.
[N:Franchomme] A cello says what a pianist only hints. I've told him so for a year.
[N:Franchomme] The boy from Cologne follows me about. Tell him to practise instead.
```
- **Sprite.** Tall, fair moustache, cello case on his back. **Liberty.** N (the compressed summer is R-57).

#### NPC-02 · Sigismond Thalberg
*8 Jan 1812 – 27 Apr 1871 · 21 · Vienna; German tours · Duel rival (BOSS-08), later GST-10; private returns Dec 1833 and Feb 1834 · CH-07 · Liberty Y · B "Thalberg" (`intent`)*

- **1830–34.** Raised in Vienna in the household of Prince Moritz Dietrichstein, generally taken to be his father; Hummel's pupil, touring the German lands since 1830 with operatic fantasies of fearsome polish. Paris debut 1835–36; the duel with Liszt 1837.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07 (Sat 7 Sep):** the embassy's private guest; Vane sponsors the duel and Brücke rigs Min-jun's action (BOSS-08). Win or lose, he tries the rigged instrument and apologises: the first outsider to see that someone wants Min-jun humiliated. **CH-14 (SQ-20):** Belgiojoso's guest; BOSS-35; GST-10. **CH-E2 (Feb 1834):** the rematch, with only REP-27–34 and *Harmonies* (LAW-25).
- *Wants:* a fair contest. *Gives:* respect, a witness to the rigging, GST-10. *Hook:* SQ-20, BOSS-35.
- **Voice.** Impeccably courteous, short formal sentences, competitive beneath; never gloats, never cheats.
```
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:intent] His Excellency's floor, your music, my hands. Begin when you like.
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:grave] Your treble was stiff as wire. Not your doing. I shall learn whose.
THALBERG  [P:NPC-02:smile] The princess has two pianos and no patience. Again, monsieur?
```
- **Sprite.** Tall, immaculate, Viennese black coat, pearl-grey waistcoat, silver watch-chain; duel idle a slow wrist roll. **Ties.** Vane's unwitting instrument, Liszt's future rival. **Liberty.** Y (R-10): the private visits and the early three-hand device; appendix LIB-08: his December programme.

#### NPC-33 · Gioachino Rossini
*29 Feb 1792 – 13 Nov 1868 · 41 · Paris · Guest of honour at the embassy; judges the Thalberg duel · CH-07 · Liberty N · B "Rossini" (`savouring`)*

- **1830–34.** No opera since *Guillaume Tell* (1829); he lives in Paris, suing for the pension the July Revolution put in doubt, famous, idle by choice, a celebrated gourmand.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07:** judges the duel; his verdict changes with the result but praises both. **CH-13 (optional):** in a box, eating sorbet through his own overture.
- *Wants:* a duel worth his supper. *Gives:* Renown, win or lose. *Hook:* the CH-13 box and its gossip line: the Baron's forges cast cannon, and Rossini prefers copper pans.
- **Voice.** Lazily witty; food as metaphor; generous verdicts.
```
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:savouring] A duel needs a judge and a supper. Tonight I am both.
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:smile] Thalberg was perfect. You were impossible. I prefer impossible.
ROSSINI  [P:NPC-33:savouring] Never trust a gala without sorbet in the second part.
```
- **Sprite.** Stout, chestnut wig, napkin tucked into his cravat, tasting from a spoon. **Ties.** Judges Min-jun, unaware of Vane's rigging. **Liberty.** N (the duel is R-10).

#### NPC-48 · Anna Liszt
*28 May 1788 – 6 Feb 1866 · 44 → 45 · Paris, rue de Provence (no. 61) · Liszt's mother, warm and practical · CH-07 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Born Anna Lager in Krems and widowed in 1827, she keeps house for her son and keeps him fed.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07+:** the flat (a sub-interior of LOC-P10) is an optional rest. **CH-14:** she mends the worn white glove Liszt will give at the Window (KEY-30), and feeds Lucile.
- *Wants:* her son fed and home before dawn. *Gives:* soup and a free inn-style rest once per chapter. *Hook:* Liszt's keepsake glove.
- **Voice.** Practical Austrian warmth; scolds with soup.
```
[N:Anna Liszt] He eats nothing before he plays and everything after. Sit. Eat now.
[N:Anna Liszt] A virtuoso with a hole in his glove? Not while I have thread.
[N:Anna Liszt] [in German] Krems is a small town. Paris is a small town with more noise.
```
- **Sprite.** Plump, white cap, grey shawl, darning in her lap. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-03 · Jacques Offenbach
*20 Jun 1819 – 5 Oct 1880 · 13 → 14 · Cologne; Paris from Nov; admitted 30 Nov · Comic guest (GST-08) · CH-12 · Liberty Y (minor: the Opéra pit) · B "Offenbach" (`cheeky`)*

- **1830–34.** Jacob, son of the Cologne cantor Isaac Offenbach, comes to Paris with his brother Julius in November 1833; Cherubini waives the rule against foreigners after hearing him.
- **Scenes.** **CH-12:** queuing at the gate, then admitted. **CH-13:** a substitute cellist in the Opéra pit who follows Chopin into the corridors "for the adventure" (GST-08 GALOP, BOSS-19). **CH-E2:** guest again; on Sat 1 Mar he carries Lucile's Salon canvas through the Louvre crowd on the BOSS-33 approach, cello case and all. Comedy never touches grief scenes; his faith is respected.
- *Wants:* adventure, and one nod from Franchomme. *Gives:* GALOP and a fourteen-year-old's nerve. *Hook:* the Franchomme signature errand (CH-E2).
- **Voice.** Cheeky, quick, self-mocking.
```
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:cheeky] Substitute cellist. Substitute for whom? Whoever's ill tonight!
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:smile] Monsieur Chopin walks like a duke. I walk like a duck. We both arrive.
OFFENBACH  [P:NPC-03:cheeky] Franchomme nodded at me. Once! I shall live on it till spring.
```
- **Sprite.** All elbows, oversized coat, a cello case taller than his shoulders. **Ties.** Chopin, Farrenc, Cherubini; fights Roussel (BOSS-19). **Liberty.** Y (R-25): hired on Franchomme's word.

#### NPC-35 · François-Antoine Habeneck
*22 Jan 1781 – 8 Feb 1849 · 52 · Opéra conductor · Yields his podium to Berlioz, sulking · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Principal conductor of the Opéra and founder (1828) of the Société des Concerts du Conservatoire, which taught Paris Beethoven's symphonies; he conducted the *Fantastique* premiere.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** Archon insists on Berlioz (R-29); Habeneck surrenders the podium and sulks in the wings. **CH-14 (Sun 15 Dec):** conducts Hiller's concert.
- *Wants:* his podium back. *Gives:* a gruff handshake for Berlioz at the CH-14 concert. *Hook:* LOC-P35.
- **Voice.** Gruff pride; tempo is law.
```
[N:Habeneck] I gave Paris its Beethoven. Tonight I give Berlioz my podium. One evening.
[N:Habeneck] Watch the bow, gentlemen, not the pianists. Pianists lie about tempo.
[N:Habeneck] Three keyboards and one orchestra. Monsieur Hiller, after you.
```
- **Sprite.** Heavy brows, a violin bow for a baton, snuffbox. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-34 · Giacomo Meyerbeer
*5 Sep 1791 – 2 May 1864 · 41 → 42 · Paris · Gala guest · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** Master of the Opéra since *Robert le diable* (1831), anxious, courteous, endlessly revising his next grand opera.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** in a box near the King's; asks Berlioz how the brass was balanced while Min-jun bites back a century of orchestration.
- *Wants:* to learn the trick of the brass. *Gives:* a public compliment (Renown). *Hook:* a CH-13 `[SLIP]` prompt (orchestration; unflagged, read by no later scene).
- **Voice.** Anxious courtesy; forever revising.
```
[N:Meyerbeer] Robert runs a third season and I still rewrite the fifth act.
[N:Meyerbeer] A concerto from Corée, Monsieur Kang. Paris will want another by spring.
[N:Meyerbeer] Your brass, Monsieur Berlioz, is too loud. Teach me how.
```
- **Sprite.** Slight, spectacles, a programme rolled tight. **Liberty.** N; his Judaism is respected.

#### NPC-38 · Adolphe Nourrit
*3 Mar 1802 – 8 Mar 1839 · 31 · Paris Opéra · Gala tenor · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The Opéra's leading tenor, creator of Arnold in *Guillaume Tell* and Robert in *Robert le diable*, a liberal who sang patriotic songs from the stage after the July days.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** sings in Part One of the gala (KEY-25) and gives Min-jun a singer's advice backstage. Min-jun knows how his life ends and says nothing.
- *Wants:* the concerto's slow theme for a song. *Gives:* breathing advice before the concerto. *Hook:* LAW-18's silence, one `{EMOTE:PC-01:...}`.
- **Voice.** A singer's warmth; thinks in breaths.
```
[N:Nourrit] I sang Robert two years running. Tonight I sing for orphans. Better.
[N:Nourrit] Breathe before the phrase, monsieur, not inside it. Pianists forget.
[N:Nourrit] Your concerto has no singer. Lend me its slow theme, afterwards.
```
- **Sprite.** Handsome, costume doublet under a greatcoat. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-37 · Marie Taglioni
*23 Apr 1804 – 22 Apr 1884 · 28 → 29 · Paris Opéra · Her *Sylphide* interlude covers the rafters fight · CH-13 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** The ballerina who made *La Sylphide* (March 1832) the sensation of Paris, rising on pointe and flying on the Opéra's wires.
- **Scenes.** **CH-13:** her scene plays below while the rafters team (DE, LU, AL) fights among the wires that carry her; afterwards she thanks them for keeping off them.
- *Wants:* the wires left alone. *Gives:* the cover of her scene. *Hook:* BOSS-18's arena, her flying rig in the flies.
- **Voice.** Cool, precise, a worker's pride.
```
[N:Mlle Taglioni] The wires take me at nine. Whatever fights up there, keep off them.
[N:Mlle Taglioni] A sylph lands without a sound. You landed like a piano.
[N:Mlle Taglioni] One flight is luck. Two are work. I fly again tomorrow.
```
- **Sprite.** White tulle and wings; a 3-frame float loop. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-39 · Narcisse Girard
*27 Jan 1797 – 16 Jan 1860 · 36 · Paris · Conducts the 22 Dec concert · CH-E2 · Liberty N · C*

- **1830–34.** A Mantes-born violinist and conductor whom Berlioz engages for his own concerts in these years.
- **Scenes.** **CH-E2 (Sun 22 Dec, afternoon):** conducts Berlioz's Conservatoire-hall concert, Paganini in a side box: the epilogue's opening scene.
- *Wants:* a clean downbeat. *Gives:* the 22 Dec concert. *Hook:* CH-E2's opening; Vasseur among the extra violins.
- **Voice.** Dry calm under pressure.
```
[N:Girard] Sunday afternoon, a full hall and Paganini in a box. Breathe, Hector.
[N:Girard] Berlioz conducts with his elbows. I conduct with my wrist. Watch my wrist.
[N:Girard] Places. The Signor is listening, and so is everyone he frightens.
```
- **Sprite.** Lean, grey temples, baton held like a pen. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-22 · Niccolò Paganini
*27 Oct 1782 – 27 May 1840 · 50 → 51 · Touring; Paris in Dec · Legend in talk; attends the 22 Dec concert · CH-E2 · Liberty N · B "Paganini" (`piercing`)*

- **1830–34.** The violinist whose Paris debut (March 1831), and Liszt's hearing of him (April 1832), changed what virtuosity meant. He attends Berlioz's concert of 22 Dec 1833, and in January 1834 asks him for a work for his Stradivari viola, the seed of *Harold en Italie*.
- **Scenes.** **CH-07–CH-14:** a legend in talk; BOSS-36 is an Echo of the legend, not the man. **CH-E2:** the side-box stare at Min-jun; in January Berlioz receives his note (KEY-37).
- *Wants:* nothing anyone in the hall can give him. *Gives:* Berlioz's commission. *Hook:* KEY-37; the legend behind BQ-PC07.
- **Voice.** Few words, each a verdict.
```
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:piercing] You. The pianist from nowhere. You listen like a thief.
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:grave] Monsieur Berlioz. In January, expect a letter. Read it twice.
PAGANINI  [P:NPC-22:neutral] Monsieur Berlioz. Your symphony. Again, and louder.
```
- **Sprite.** Gaunt, long black hair, loose black coat, utterly still. **Liberty.** N; the 1838 kneeling is never shown (R-53).

#### NPC-01 · César Franck
*10 Dec 1822 – 8 Nov 1890 · 10 → 11 · Liège · Prodigy on the Rhine road · CH-11 · Liberty N · B "César Franck" (`shy`)*

- **1830–34.** A pupil of the Liège music school since about 1830, whose father will tour him in 1834–35 and move the family to Paris in 1835 (R-22). Never in Paris in this game.
- **Scenes.** **CH-11:** plays nightly on the Hôtel de la Meuse's salon piano. Chopin, who joins the tour here and was a celebrated child prodigy himself, though a gently raised one, asks the father to let the boy stop. Min-jun knows what César will become and says nothing.
- *Wants:* to stop. *Gives:* Chopin a mirror. *Hook:* a Kindred scene for Chopin (no BP: he is R10 from CH-09).
- **Voice.** Shy, polite, longing to stop.
```
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:shy] Monsieur Chopin, will you tell Papa that I may stop now?
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:smile] When I'm grown I want an instrument with more keys. Many more.
CÉSAR  [P:NPC-01:shy] Papa says the Hummel is for gentlemen. Are you gentlemen?
```
- **Sprite.** Small, round-faced, a Sunday collar too stiff. **Liberty.** N.

#### NPC-49 · Nicolas-Joseph Franck
*1794 – 1871 · ~39 · Liège · César's driving father · CH-11 · Liberty N · C "M. Franck"*

- **1830–34.** A clerk with large ambitions for his musical sons César and Joseph.
- **Scenes.** **CH-11:** sells seats to hear César and asks the visitors to write to Paris about him.
- *Wants:* a letter of praise sent to Paris. *Gives:* seats at a franc. *Hook:* Chopin's quiet rebuke.
